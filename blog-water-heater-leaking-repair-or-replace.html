Water Heater Leaking: Repair or Replace?

A leaking water heater does not always mean the entire unit must be replaced. The source of the water matters. A loose connection or valve problem is different from a tank that is corroding or leaking through the body of the unit.

Start by identifying where the water appears

Water can show up around supply connections, the temperature-and-pressure relief valve, the drain valve, nearby plumbing, condensation, or the tank itself. Because water can travel across the top or base of the unit, the wettest spot is not always the exact source.

If water is spreading quickly, near electrical components, or creating a slip or property-damage risk, treat the situation as urgent and contact qualified help.

Some leaks may be repairable

A professional may be able to repair or replace a leaking fitting, connection, valve, or other serviceable component depending on the equipment and condition.

The right repair depends on the source and whether the tank and surrounding components remain in serviceable condition.

When replacement becomes more likely

A leak from the tank body, significant corrosion, repeated problems, or an older unit with multiple failing components can shift the conversation toward replacement. A professional can help distinguish a serviceable leak from a tank failure.

Do not seal or patch a pressurized water-heater tank as a substitute for proper evaluation.

Maintenance can help reveal problems earlier

EPA WaterSense recommends regular water-heater maintenance and notes that homeowners should visually inspect for leaks, corrosion, aged gaskets, or loose connections. It also recommends professional service at least annually when appropriate for the system.

Follow the manufacturer instructions for your specific unit, especially before draining, testing valves, or working around gas or electrical connections.

What to share when requesting plumbing help

Provide the heater type, approximate age if known, where the water appears, whether the leak is constant, and whether hot-water performance has changed. A photo of the data label can help identify model and capacity.

Frequently asked questions

Does a leaking water heater always need replacement?

No. Some leaks come from serviceable connections or valves. A tank-body leak or serious corrosion is more likely to lead to replacement.

Is a water heater leak an emergency?

It can be if water is spreading quickly, contacting electrical equipment, causing property damage, or the source cannot be controlled safely.

Should I drain a leaking water heater myself?

Follow the manufacturer instructions and only perform steps you can do safely. Gas, electricity, hot water, and pressure can create hazards, so professional help may be appropriate.
